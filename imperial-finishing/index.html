FAQ

Common questions

What affects the cost of finishing carpentry?+

Mostly the amount of linear footage or number of pieces, the profile and material (paint-grade MDF versus stain-grade hardwood), how many corners and cuts are involved, and how level and square the walls are. A walkthrough gives you an exact number. The trim estimator above gives a rough material count to start with.

How soon can you start, and how long does a job take?+

Baseboards and casing for a typical room often take a day or less; a full kitchen of cabinets usually takes several days. Start dates depend on the current schedule.

Confirm: current lead time

Do you supply the materials, or can I buy my own?+

Confirm with Mike Many finish carpenters can do either. Supplying materials lets the carpenter pick stock that is straight and matched, while buying your own can suit a specific product you already chose.

Paint-grade or stain-grade: what's the difference?+

Paint-grade trim is usually MDF or finger-jointed pine that gets filled, caulked and painted. Stain-grade trim is solid wood like oak, chosen for its grain, so joints have to be tight because there is no caulk to hide them. Try both looks with the trim finish buttons on the 3D room.

Do I need a permit for finishing work?+

Trim, doors, cabinets and built-ins generally do not need a building permit on their own. Permits usually come into play when walls, structure, plumbing or electrical are changing as part of the wider renovation.
